### 第二封短信怎么读（主持人的解读，待她确认）

- **"After transformation or after government doing transfer will get to one equilibrium"**：一次结构性变化（transformation，含 AI 本身带来的变化），或一次政策干预（转移支付之类），之后经济会走到一个**新的**均衡。这是对"理解二"（罕见冲击 → 模型彻底失效）的直接回应：冲击不是终点，是过渡。若 "government doing transfer" 另有所指（你们此前聊过的某件事），以你们的上下文为准。
- **"but always dynamic stochastic"**：那个均衡不是一个点，而是一个随机过程——宏观意义上的 dynamic stochastic equilibrium（有平稳分布的随机稳态）。这意味着**遍历性在每一个 regime 之内是回来的**：过渡之后，环境又成为渐近遍历的，可以被学到。
- **"AI plus human society can achieve full rational expectation Bayesian correlated equilibrium"**：AI 与人一起构成学习系统。"Bayesian correlated equilibrium"（Aumann 1987：相关均衡即贝叶斯理性 + 共同先验；Bergemann–Morris 2016 的 Bayes correlated equilibrium 处理不完全信息）在这里的自然解读是：AI 是共享的信号 / 相关装置，许多人依据同一套 AI 建议行动，每个人的信念在给定自己信息下是正确的（"full rational expectations"）。
- **"constant evolving"**：transformation 不断到来，均衡是一串而不是一个；每次过渡之后系统重新学到新的均衡。
- **"but that is good thing, not bad"**：她把"不断演化"看成进步本身，不是风险；与她录音里的"不要太悲观、有竞争、要独立"（01:02:38–01:03:52）一致。
- **合起来**：她不否认三点理解描述的现象，但把它们**限定在过渡期**——旧规律已失效、新规律尚未学到的那一段——并认为 AI + 人会把过渡走完。于是澄清信的任务从"请她确认三点"变成"请她把自己放在'主持人的谨慎'与'她的演化均衡观'之间的哪里"，以及一个新问题：在人人依 AI 信号行动的相关均衡里，**让均衡朝新真相演化的新信息从哪里来**（这正是"理解三"换成她的语言）。

### 她的立场，合起来是三句话

1. **参数形式是外推的代价。** 想回答数据之外的问题（15 年后的脉冲响应、全新产品的定价、没试过的政策），必须有 functional form；而结论随 form 而变，且恰恰在最需要它的地方（支撑之外）数据无法检验它。她**不是反对参数模型**，反对的是"随便做假设"：假设要么从模型推出来（Hansen 的教训），要么先看数据再定（Hildenbrand 的做法）。
2. **非参数的代价不是"抓不住动态"，而是两条边界。** (a) 只能在数据支撑附近说话，推远了就是宽到没人信的置信带，或者无话可说；(b) 要从一条历史里学到规律，数据必须渐近平稳 / 遍历（00:20:41），否则学不到——而经济里"人的行为在变"（00:51–00:52），这个前提本身可疑。在支撑之内、前提成立时，非参数方法（包括神经网络）随数据增多逼近真相：这正是她的筛分法纲领，也是来信"AI 在渐近遍历环境里像理性预期的人"的根据。
3. **AI 落在哪里。** 大神经网络 = 极其灵活的非参数筛，训练于近乎全部已有数据 → 支撑之内（短期预测、邻域外推）极强。被问到支撑之外的问题时它照样回答，而答案背后必然有某个 functional form——藏在结构与训练里、没人选择、没人能检视——或者根本没有依据。"一层 assumption behind"指的就是这层看不见的假设；黑箱把参数世界的风险（形式错）和非参数世界的诚实（无话可说）一起掩盖了。

### 对主持人理解的校正

- "用参数估计的风险在于我们不知道世界模型、假设可能错得危险" —— **对**，是她的终身主题（00:08、00:15、00:49）。措辞上她说的是 "sensitive"、"全变了"；"危险"是合理引申。补一句：她反对的是未经推导、未经数据检视的假设，不是参数模型本身。
- "非参数估计极其困难，因为不理解 DGP、不知道数据如何演化、抓不住动态" —— **需修正**。非参数正是在不知道 DGP 形式时的选择；它的困难是"不能出支撑"和"需要渐近平稳 / 遍历"。"不知道数据如何演化"对，但要说准：是**非平稳**（规律本身在变）让它学不到，不是它抓不住动态。维数灾难她这次没讲，不要替她补。

## 4. 三点理解逐条核对

### 理解一：真正的危险是我们对强大的 AI 过于自满，以至于相信自己就生活在那个渐近遍历的子空间里

- **需澄清**：危险在用模型的**人**、不在模型本身，她是否同意？她是否把这列为首要危险？

### 理解二：用这个 AI 模型去认识世界，一旦罕见冲击来临，模型会彻底失效

- **录音支持**：00:46:23–00:46:44、00:48:55–00:49:06、00:49:45、01:00:51–01:01:08。
- **两处要校准**：
  1. **"罕见"应改为"前所未见 / 改变运动规律"**。在渐近遍历的世界里，罕见冲击仍在平稳分布之内，看过全部历史的 AI 见过它的同类；真正要命的是**支撑之外**的冲击（全新产品、未试过的政策），或**规律本身在变**（行为随时间、随政策、随预测而变）。这对应她的两条理由：00:46（支撑之外）与 00:51–00:52（人在变）。
  2. **失效的方式**："哑火"（置信带宽到没人信、或无话可说）还是"**无声失败**"（照样给出自信的答案）？她 01:03:12 的"糊弄人很容易"暗示后者，但没明说。她的原话更接近"没有识别 → 无话可说"，而大模型总会给出答案——这才是"要非常小心"的所在。

## 6. 给陈老师的澄清信（英文稿，已按她的第二封短信改写，可直接发）

Dear Xiaohong,

Thank you again for last night, and for your two notes since. Before I write up the AI part of our conversation, I want to be sure I understand your position precisely, so that nothing is attributed to you that you did not mean. Below is my reading; please correct anything that is off. A yes/no plus a line is enough.

1. Your terms, so that I can explain them to readers. (a) "Asymptotically ergodic environment": the law of motion is stable and a long enough history reveals it, so a learner with enough data and compute converges to the truth; the limit of such learning is a rational-expectations agent; today's AI, with nearly all recorded data plus the models and the compute, approximates such a learner. Around minute 20 of the recording you say that the nonparametric learning in your dissertation requires asymptotically stationary data, otherwise one cannot learn, and your 1998 JET paper with Hal White is about such learning with feedback. Is your first note that idea applied to AI? (b) Your second note: after a transformation, or after a policy intervention such as a transfer, the economy moves to a new equilibrium, which is dynamic and stochastic rather than a fixed point; AI plus human society can learn it, reaching a fully rational-expectations Bayesian correlated equilibrium that keeps evolving as new transformations arrive. Have I read it right? By "Bayesian correlated equilibrium", do you have in mind AI as the shared signal, the correlation device whose recommendations many agents act on, with everyone's beliefs correct given what they know?

2. Putting the two notes together. My reading: the world is not ergodic once and for all, but it is ergodic regime by regime. Within a regime the AI-plus-human system learns the law and behaves like rational-expectations agents; a transformation breaks the old law; the system learns the new one; the sequence of equilibria is the "constant evolving", and that evolution is good because it is how progress happens. Is this your view? If so, the risk I had in mind lives in the transition: the period after a transformation when the old law no longer holds and the new one has not yet been learned, during which a model trained on the old regime still answers confidently. Would you accept "the danger lives in the transition, and in mistaking the current regime for a permanent one", rather than my earlier "the model fails profoundly when a rare shock hits"? If you agree, I would present your outlook as the answer to my worry, and my three worries as the conditions under which it holds: humans stay in the loop as learners, transitions are treated with humility, and independent action keeps generating data from the new regime.

3. Where the danger sits. My formulation: not with AI but with the user of the model who forgets the condition and treats the current regime as the world; the danger sits with the user rather than with the model. Fair? And "AI plus human society" seems essential to you: humans are part of the learning system, not its audience. Is that the sense of "humans need to evolve with AI"?

4. Exploration inside a correlated equilibrium. One question your second note raises for me. When many agents act on the same AI signals, where does the new information come from that lets the equilibrium evolve toward the new truth after a transformation: independent human action and experimentation, exogenous shocks, or experiments run by the AI itself? Last night you said that an experiment is simply collecting data that did not exist before, that a truth not in the sample cannot be recovered by prompting, and that people should stay independent and not follow the crowd. Is "stay independent" the condition that keeps the evolving equilibrium learning rather than locking in on AI-generated data? And is the loop you raised, content and behavior increasingly produced by AI, the feedback structure of your 1998 paper at the scale of an economy? You were less pessimistic than I was about it: competition among AIs, someone will notice. Should I record your position as "a risk to watch, not a bottleneck"?

5. Parametric versus nonparametric. My reading of that thread, from Hildenbrand's class and the IO lectures where a small change of functional form flipped every conclusion, through Lars Hansen's "derive the mixing condition from the model, do not assume it", to last night's structural VAR example: a functional form is what lets a model answer questions far from the data, such as a long-horizon impulse response, a radically new product, or an untried policy, but the conclusion is then hostage to the form, which the data cannot check where it matters most. Going nonparametric removes that risk but confines you to the neighbourhood of the data, and only under asymptotic stationarity. A large neural network is a very flexible nonparametric estimator trained on nearly all data: excellent inside the support, but when asked an out-of-support question it still answers, with a functional form hidden in the architecture and the training that nobody chose and nobody can inspect. Is that what you meant by "there is always a layer of assumptions behind"?

6. Two things I would like to write, with your permission. (a) You said last night that you never believed real people have rational expectations; you did not know your own plans.
